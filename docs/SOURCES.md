# Sources for the recovery parameters

The recovery lens is a heuristic. This file records what each default in `five3one/params.py` rests on. The pass covered 21 primary papers: 4 read in full text, 17 as abstracts or abstract-level records. Samples are small (9 to 26) and almost all are trained young men.

Labels used in `params.py`:

- **SOURCED:** the number comes from published data.
- **DIRECTION-SOURCED:** the direction comes from published data; the size is a judgement.
- **JUDGEMENT:** no evidence found; a stated assumption.

## What the evidence supports

- **Proximity to failure dominates.** With load and total reps matched, sets stopped at half the possible reps were recovered within 6 hours while sets to failure took 48 to 72 hours [1]. At 24 hours, failure and 1 rep in reserve were both about 3% down on bar velocity and 3 reps in reserve was back to baseline [6].
- **Set count matters less than proportionally.** Doubling non-failure sets did not lengthen recovery [1]. Across studies, 3, 4, 6 and 8 sets to failure span roughly 48 to 96 hours [1][5][6][7][8]. No within-study comparison of set numbers at matched effort was found.
- **High-rep volume costs more than heavy low-rep work as normally done.** 8x10 at 70% left isometric strength 11% down at 72 hours; 8x3 at 90% produced no significant change [5]. Volume and effort were not matched, so load itself is not shown to be protective.
- **No muscle-specific split is well supported.** In well-trained men, squat, bench press and deadlift recovered on a similar time course [7]; pectoralis and triceps showed similar stress after bench pressing [12]. Single-joint elbow-flexor work cost more than rows [11], which is an exercise effect. The limb differences in [9] come from untrained men doing maximal eccentrics.
- **Training status.** The same relative session impaired performance for longer before a training programme than after it [4]; untrained men lost more power than trained peers [18]. Nothing was found on detraining specifically or on energy deficit and recovery time.
- **Stacking exercises.** No study was found on whether several exercises for one muscle in a session cost more than their sum. Within a session the second of two exercises for the same muscle lost about 20% of its reps [20].
- **Long muscle length.** Supported only in eccentric-only protocols [21]; no evidence for ordinary lifts in trained people.
- **Soreness is not the recovery signal.** It correlates poorly with loss of muscle function [22].
- **Fractional sets.** Counting indirect sets as 0.5 predicted hypertrophy and strength gains better than counting them as 1 or 0 [14]. Only those three values were compared, and the study concerns adaptation; using 0.5 for recovery dose is this tool's assumption.

## Gaps

Energy deficit, compounding across exercises, muscle length in ordinary lifts, and recovery of the spinal erectors and hamstrings after deadlifts have no usable evidence. The top dose band is extrapolated.
